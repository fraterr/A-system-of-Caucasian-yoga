## Swastika and Souwastika

> Oldest symbol — Swastika represents contracting of the heart, while Souwastica — expaning. Dividing the work in mysticism and occultism and magick into two-distinct paths.— I (first — doctrine of the closed heart — but open mind — developing reason — it is symbol of Souwastica used in the Orient,) II (second — doctrine of the open heart — and feelings, brings in wisdom — it is symbol of Swastika. Used in Oxident)
> <cite>ms. p. 53</cite>

The author adds that a cross-section of the heart "will show the muscles in the form of Swastika and Souwastika". In his drawing the Souwastika's arms bend counter-clockwise and the Swastika's clockwise. The Doctrine of the Heart, which follows, belongs to the second path: "the doctrine of the open heart".

## The process of creation

At the end of the Creation Exercise the author reads three emblems as pictures of the energy rising from the solar plexus to the crown:

> Symbollically represented process of creation is in Caduceus of Hermes with the two serpents encircling it mounting toward pine cone or Mercury's [heat?] with wings spread attached to it. Also winged scarab of the Egyptians, skarab representing human skull. In Orient a skull, the same symbol often used in Christian symbolic art. Mercury (Hermes) carrying demand or order on the wings of will.
> <cite>ms. p. 59</cite>

The "pine cone" at the top of the caduceus is the pineal; the "Humaskull" of the Index is the skull as the vessel the energy reaches. The same rising force is "the serpent within the spinal column" (Fourth Lesser Arcane), "the serpent power of the body (Kundalini in Yoga)", and the reason for the name "Order of the Serpent" in the Thirteenth Arcane.

## The locked throat

"Locking the throat is represented as Crux Ansata in Egypt noose in India-Tibet, tie that binds in Persia e.t.c." The Index adds "noose conscious, subconscious, superconscious". "The tie that binds" returns in the Illumination chapter as the invisible threads connecting a Master's ego to the egos within its scope.

## Ka and Ba, the Silver Cord

The feeling "as if a bird was clasped to the back of the skull" in the Sixth Arcane "is the Ka or bird (Ba) of the Egyptian mythology", and the sense of "swelling or expansion of aura" is its spreading. In dream consciousness "the Silver Cord will guide you back to your abode of flesh".

## Objects of power

Rods of hardened coal (Sun) and lodestone (Moon); the "mysterious metal (bronze) Auricallum… composed of five metals, each having a definite color — white, black, red, blue and yellow", "electron" of gold and silver, and the planetary metals ☉ gold, ☾ silver, ♄ lead, ♃ tin, ♂ iron, ♀ copper, ☿ mercury; Babylonian maces with horned heads; the Tibetan dorgee. The magical wand: hollow wood, a magnetised steel wire with the north pole in the hand, a tourmaline at the tip, copper wire in a right-handed spiral.

## Emblems drawn without comment

The manuscript is punctuated with small drawings the text does not explain: the crowned, globe-bellied creature on the cover; the seal with the pentagram and eye and the great S-sigil on the Key to Mastery page; the sun, hexagram and crescent closing the Sixteenth Arcane; the winged disc closing the Eighth; the vortex-cup between a cone and a dome before the Thirteenth; the tree with its roots wrapped round a seed at the end of Illumination; and the therefore-sign ∴ used throughout as a paragraph mark.
