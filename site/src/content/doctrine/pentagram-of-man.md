## The plate

The Compendium gives the pentagram a full page: a white star on a black disc, framed by its names:

> ∴ Star which is the crown, the power, the love, the five points of fellowship. Star of ressurection. ∴ The star of man. The star of saviour. The star of senses. The morning star. The brightest star. The star of wise men. The [star?] of elements. ∴
>
> Pentagram of the man (microcosmos mirroring the macrocosmos), the secret of the sacred mastery and supremacy.
> <cite>ms. p. 46</cite>

"Microcosmos mirroring the macrocosmos" is the traditional reading of the five-pointed star as the human figure (head and four limbs), and it is echoed in the Illumination chapter: "The microcosmos putting on the robe of the macrocosmos, the mystery of the great in the small and the small in the great."

## Where it appears in the book

- **The seal on the Key to Mastery page**: a small circular seal with a pentagram and an eye at its centre, ringed with script.
- **In the heart.** At the climax of the Doctrine of the Heart, "you enter the interior of the heart, filled with red clouds and mist, in the center is standing the arc, with measures of discrimination, and over the arc you see the blasing pentagram, with the all seing eye in the center." The drawing labels the heart Love / Al / Im / Peace.
- **In the air.** The Third Great Arcane builds the star with the pointing index finger, one syllable of Yat-Ha-Ah-Hu-Vo per stroke, encloses it in a circle and a spiral, and thrusts through the centre. Drawn to the four quarters, above and below, "using only the pentagram without the spiral thrust", it is "the wall of absolute protection"; with the thrust it is "a powerful and terrible weapon to strike and destroy the enemies".

## The strokes

From the diagram on ms. p. 61, the star is drawn beginning at the lower left, up to the apex, down to the lower right, up to the upper left, across to the upper right, and down to the starting point: the sequence 1–2–3–4–5 of the labelled "motions". The sixth motion is the circle and inner half-circle (a spiral "from left to right"), and the final plate shows the "Complete with Zend mantram", the star ringed with the Avestan-style script.

## The five points of fellowship and the eye

Two of the star's epithets are Masonic ("the five points of fellowship", "the all-seeing eye"), two are scriptural ("the morning star", "the star of wise men"), and the author's own reading is "the star of man… of senses… of elements". He does not develop the correspondences; the star is used as an emblem of the whole human being and, in the ritual, as a shape that the will draws.
