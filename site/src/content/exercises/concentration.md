## In brief

The Seventh Lesser Arcane is the system's method of concentration. After a full First Master Arcane you take one simple, concrete thought, turn the eyes inward and upward until a slight pressure appears between the eyebrows, and press the thought into that point, holding, repeating and splitting it. The text defines a ladder of attention, concentration, meditation and contemplation, and says that on this path "the Light" appears, "so bright, that the light of the Sun will seem only a shadow in comparison with it".

## Technique

1. **Sit** erect, spine straight, facing the position of the Sun.
2. **Do the complete First Master Arcane.** (Filling in from that card: twelve Master breaths of 7 s in, 1 s hold, 7 s out, 1 s hold, three each on red, yellow, blue and white — 3 min 12 s.)
3. **Formulate the thought** on which you want to concentrate. Make it simple and concrete, then hold it in your attention.
4. **Turn the eyes** inward and as far upward as you can comfortably reach, and close them as much as you comfortably can.
5. **Feel the pressure.** Inside the forehead, between the eyebrows, you will feel a slight pressure or tension, at times a slight pinching. Hold this feeling and do not let it relax.
6. **Forget the breath and the body.** The text says that at times, after an exhalation or in the middle of one, you will comfortably stop breathing, and that "this should be so".
7. **Press the thought into the point** where you feel the pressure (the manuscript's verb is unclear; the transcriber reads "squeeze"). Hold it there with the pressure, repeat it in front of the point and inside it, "on all the modes and manners"; halve it and split it — both the thought and the words it contains. This, the text says, is called careful observation.
8. **Look for the light.** During meditation and contemplation "always look for the light, and it will come to you".

## What the text says it develops

- The stages the author defines: **attention**; **concentration**, the riveting of attention on the object, "also called setting the heart on the object"; and becoming at one with the object, which is **meditation** when the subject is of spiritual importance and **contemplation** when it belongs to worldly life. Each higher stage begins with concentration.
- **Illumination, "the Light"**: brighter than the Sun, real, and "to be seen on every plane – physical, mental, spiritual and psychic".
- **The clairvoyant state**: in a passive state "you will see the things, objects, persons, and happenings and events".
- The chapter's closing summary lists what the arcane and its auxiliaries give: "concentration, meditation, contemplation, splitting of the ether, inward sight, far away gaze, clairvoyance, thought form reading, aura, and psychometry".

## Phenomena and cautions

- At certain times you will see light before the eyes: "a dot a star a eye, a vision of Heavenly Father, a Guardian Angel etc." The text assigns these to the second stage, not to concentration itself.
- The breath may stop by itself; the pressure may feel like pinching and is to be held, not released.
- The author's only limits are comfort in turning and closing the eyes.

## Notes for the reader

- The eye position is the one given for the Sixth Lesser Arcane (the Aum): eyes lifted and converging toward the "urna" between the eyebrows, "looking on the mountain tops".
- A bracketed gloss after the definition of meditation — "{(called perform ∴ „Lahui Yama[?]}" — is not fully legible and is left as it stands.
- The concentration itself uses no colour or word; the only breath and colour work is the First Master Arcane.
- The eye and gazing exercises that follow in the chapter are given as separate auxiliary cards: sun gazing and the after-image, eye muscle exercises, splitting the ether, inner and far-away gaze, crystal gazing and thought-form reading, sky gazing. The "(x)" in the closing summary refers to the telepathy note on the crystal-gazing card.
