## In brief

Mullah (or Mullha) Mudra is "breathing trough the rectum". Kneeling in prostration, forehead on the earth and hips raised, you insert a short polished tube past the sphincters and breathe deeply with the chest: as the chest fills, air is pushed out of the large intestine through the tube, and as it empties, air is drawn in. The author gives it for "the developement for hibernation", for rejuvenation and for cleaning the intestinal tract and lower bowel, and says it "makes away with the indigestions and constipation".

## Technique

1. **Prepare the tube** beforehand: bamboo, hard rubber, wood, ivory or another suitable substance, about 5–6 in long and ½ in in diameter, with a bore of ⅛–¼ in, the ends very well rounded and polished. It may be slightly bent. Keep it clean.
2. **Face the Sun** (or its direction). Go down on the knees, straighten the spine with arms and hands up, looking slightly upward. Relax.
3. **Inhale** strongly and fully, swaying slightly backwards.
4. **Holding the breath, bend forward** until you can bend the arms at the elbows and rest elbows and forearms on the earth. Swing on elbows and knees until the forehead touches the ground comfortably — "this is called prostrating oneself". The elbows should be 12–24 in from the knees.
5. **Release the breath** the moment the elbows strike the earth. Relax.
6. **Insert the tube** into the anus, past the external and internal sphincters (about 3 in). When it is correctly placed, a passage for air is open and "you will hear a specific hissing sound of air passing to and from the large intestine".
7. **Adjust** by swaying forward and backward on elbows and knees until the passage is regulated.
8. **Breathe**: stomach relaxed, evenly and rhythmically, using mostly the chest muscles, in deep inhalations and exhalations. During the inhalation air is expelled from the intestines; during the exhalation air is drawn in through the rectum.
9. **Occasionally close the larynx** and perform the muscular act of breathing without circulating air in the lungs.
10. **For comfort**, move the hands near each other and rest the forehead or face on the knuckles; or, according to need, rise upright on the knees, arms and hands up, bending backward and inhaling deeply as at the start.
11. **Finish** with care that the air is expelled: take a deep inhalation and remove the tube while holding the breath and pushing downward (tensing).

## Timing

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Kneeling, inhalation, bend into prostration | one breath |

| Removal of the tube | one held breath |

## What the text says it develops

- "The developement for hibernation" (it follows Kechara Mudra, which is given the same purpose).
- Rejuvenation and cleaning of the intestinal tract and lower bowel.
- "Very healing" in cases of an illegibly written intestinal complaint and of the appendix.
- Strengthening of the intestines; doing away with indigestion and constipation.

## Phenomena and cautions

- The hissing sound of air passing to and from the large intestine is the sign that the tube is correctly placed.
- The reversed direction of the air — out of the bowel on the in-breath, in on the out-breath — is described as the normal working of the exercise.
- The author's own cautions: rounded, polished, clean tube; relaxed stomach; and the closing procedure that expels the air before the tube is withdrawn.

## Notes for the reader

- The word for the condition the exercise is said to heal is unreadable in the manuscript and transcribed as "[gastrms?]".
- The heading spells the name both ways, "Mullah Mudra: Mullha Mudra".
- The Twelfth Arcane (face and head rejuvenation) is described by the author as "complmentory to the XIth Arcane".
- The text does not say how the rectal breathing relates to the Master rhythm; it asks only for even, rhythmic, deep chest breathing.
