## In brief

Having given the kneeling exercise for the head, the author generalises it: "to renew and clean the tissues in different parts of the body you have to learn the way of sending the blood to them, and also to withdraw it." He gives four means: the position of the part, the phase of the breath, pressure on the vessels, and muscular tension.

## Technique

1. **By position.** To flush a part with blood, place its centre below the rest of the body; it fills. To drain it, place its centre higher than the rest.
2. **By the breath.** On inhalation, the text says, the blood recedes from the parts; while the breath is held and during exhalation it "circulates stronger". The rush and withdrawal of blood to and from the head is "absolutely synchronic with the breath".
3. **By pressing an artery.** The flow to the part it serves stops; what blood is left drains out through the veins and the part is left bloodless.
4. **By pressing a vein.** The outflow is checked while the arteries keep filling the part.
5. **Study** the places where arteries and veins lie close under the skin, so that ebb and flow can be controlled by pressing.
6. **By muscular tension.** Tensing muscles through which vessels pass pinches and contracts the arteries and veins.

## What the text says it develops

- The ability to renew and clean the tissues of any part by deliberately flooding it with arterial blood and then withdrawing it.
- The author's reasoning: arterial blood under pressure reaches the capillaries and cells, feeds them and brings the oxygen that burns waste into a form easy to eliminate; the wastes pass into the venous blood and leave through lungs, skin, kidneys, bowels and liver.

## Notes for the reader

- One word in the sentence on draining is uncertain ("put the [center?] of the part … higher"); the sense follows from the preceding sentence.
- The kneeling exercise of the parent card is the worked example of rule 1, and Mullah Mudra uses the same alternation.
