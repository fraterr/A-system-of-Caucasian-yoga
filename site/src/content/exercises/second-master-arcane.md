## In brief

The Second Master Arcane performs the twelve breaths of the First Arcane standing: a first posture flat-footed with one foot forward, then a second "on risen toes". The Index summarises it as "tension and relaxation, balance"; the text says it "teaches us how to face friends and enemies, how to defend and aggress" and "develops clairaudience and harmony". A whole manuscript page of the description is missing; see the notes.

## Technique

1. **Stand** erect, arms hanging at the sides, eyes level on the Sun, the Moon or the spot. (Filled in from the drawing and from the author's standing "sign of the Master System": spine straight, shoulders back, chest forward, head up, chin in, gaze level.)
2. **Note your breath.** Place the **right foot forward** if you are in Sun breath (right nostril), the **left foot forward** if in Moon breath (left nostril). The forward foot makes a slight angle with the other, as in the drawing.
3. **Exhale all breath** before beginning, using — the sentence breaks off here, at the foot of the last surviving page before the gap.
4. **Breathe the twelve Master breaths** as in the First Arcane (7 s in, 1 s hold, 7 s out, 1 s hold): three on red, three on yellow, three on blue, three on white, with the same expansions and the same body regions for each colour. The text at the resumption says only "…second, then begin again two more breaths on red… using same expansions as described in First Master Arcane".
5. **Rise on the toes** for the "second posture". The text says "second posture will be on risen toes" and later calls the exercise "standing and rising on the balls of the feet", but never says at which breaths you rise. See the notes.

## What the text says it develops

- "Clairaudience and harmony"; the Index adds "hearing" and "balance".
- It "teaches us how to face friends and enemies, how to defend and aggress".
- The great toe "is connected with hearing (auditory nerve) and coordinates harmony and rhythm in the body"; the text passes straight from this Arcane into the care of the feet.

## Phenomena and cautions

No phenomena survive for this Arcane. The only warnings are the general one, to exhale all breath first, and the foot-care note: keep the great toe massaged and stretched so that numbness does not set in.

## Notes for the reader

- **Manuscript page 19 is missing** from the facsimile, with the heading, the posture description and the first red breath. Page 18 ends "before starting on any of the Master Arcanes first exhale all breath using…" and page 20 resumes mid-sentence. Steps 1 to 3 are reconstructed from the drawing, the Index, the Master System posture (manuscript page 48) and the First Arcane; they are not the author's words for this exercise.
- The **second posture on risen toes** is named but never described. Whether the exercise is done twice (flat, then on the toes), whether one alternates within the twelve breaths, or whether the rise falls on the exhalation is not stated. The Compendium (manuscript page 95) confirms the shape: "standing and rising on the balls of the feet".
- The author notes that the same standing posture "is in Egyptian ritual".
- The Ninth Lesser Arcane (recharging nervous energy) uses "the Second Master Arcane exercise" with two rods of power in the hands. The foot-care note that follows has its own card.
