## In brief

"Besides the feet pay very strict attention to the hands." The author gives a short routine whose visible aim is the thumbs: "this exercise develops the thumbs, and developing them grows and strengthens the will power." He then explains what each finger stands for and how the hand should and should not be held.

## Technique

1. **Bend the arms** at the elbows and let the hands go absolutely limp, perfectly flexible at the wrists.
2. **Shake the hands** by moving forearms and arms, up and down and then in circles, so fast that the hands blur to the sight, until on stopping you feel "the strong vibration in your hands, comparable to the electric current".
3. **Rub the hands** strongly together in every way.
4. **Bend** the fingers and palms inwards and outwards, pressing them together.
5. **Stretch the thumbs.** With the fingers pressed together, move the thumbs away from them and, by pressing, widen the gap between thumbs and fingers (the drawing shows the hands palm to palm).

## What the text says it develops

- The thumbs, and through them "the will power".
- Mastery of "the motions and relations between themselves of all the fingers".

## The meaning of the fingers

In the author's terms: the thumb is logic and will power; the index finger destiny (command); the middle finger the teacher's, "used in drawing on the sand, and for explaining"; the third finger humanitarian love, compassion and altruism; the little finger sex, lust, physical love. Fingers pressed together emphasise their attributes, unless folded against the palm, when the extended fingers are the ones to read. He advises studying the hands of others.

## Phenomena and cautions

- The vibration after shaking is the sign to stop.
- Do not stick out the little finger; never fold the thumb under the fingers in a fist.

## Notes for the reader

- One word is illegible in the sentence on folded fingers.
- The doctrine of the fingers returns in the magnetic-electric exercise and in laying on of hands.
