## In brief

The second of the two exercises for redistributing the spinal fluid. A particular hand-lock is placed at the junction of head and neck and the head is rocked forward and back against the light pressure of the fingers. The text says it has "a great influence on medulla oblongata or pineal gland, developing its size and capacity", and that the hissing heard afterwards is the spinal fluid percolating to and from the fourth ventricle.

## Technique

1. **Join the hands** palms up, fingers bent so that the backs of the fingers touch, the tips of the thumbs pressed against each other (left figure in the diagram).
2. **Lift** the joined hands over the head and back, without separating the fingers, so that the ends of the bent fingers rest on the spot where the head joins the neck. The middle finger should be right on the spot, the others alongside.
3. **Press lightly** with the fingers against that point.
4. **Lower the head** to rest the chin on the chest, then lift it and move it up and back, keeping the pressure of fingers and hands throughout.
5. **Repeat** the movement twelve times or more.
6. **Posture**: stand or sit, spine erect.

## What the text says it develops

- The size and capacity of the medulla oblongata (which the author also calls the pineal gland).
- "Expansion of energies": it "can and should be done for the developement according to the need".

## Phenomena and cautions

- During or immediately after, a hissing sound of vibration at the base of the skull, explained as the spinal fluid moving to and from the fourth ventricle. No cautions are given.

## Notes for the reader

- The manuscript writes "move your hand over your head"; the diagram shows both hands joined, and this card follows the diagram.
- A word in the posture note is unreadable ("you can stand or sit, with [?] the spine erect").
- The author says the Order of the Serpent exercise that follows cannot be understood without practising this one and the jaw exercise.
