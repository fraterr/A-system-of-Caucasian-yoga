## In brief

Kechara Mudra is, in the author's words, "the process of swallowing the tongue or of inserting the tongue past soft palate into nasal cavities". It is given as the preparation for hibernation — "going into letargic trance" — and as a way of centring the life energies in the head by closing the two currents of the body individually. The whole practice is a slow, daily lengthening of the tongue by pulling and massage, until, the text says, real perfection is reached when the tongue can touch the point between the eyebrows.

## Technique

1. **Every day**, for a certain time, **stick out the tongue**.
2. **Take hold of it** with the hand through a piece of cloth, so that it does not slip from the fingers.
3. **Pull the tongue out**, gradually making it longer. The text insists: "you must pull it".
4. **Massage it** at the same time with a milking motion.
5. **The frenulum.** During the pulling the membrane under the tongue ("froenulum lingude") will, the author says, "becom loosened or cut on the teeth, permitting the tongue to gradually become longer". He adds that the cutting of the frenulum "may be done by operation, knife, or using a sharp blade of grass".
6. **Salt.** "Always after injuring the tongue trough pulling apply the salt to the wound."
7. **The goal.** Real perfection is reached when you can touch the point between the eyebrows with the tongue; the mudra proper is then the insertion of the tongue past the soft palate into the nasal cavities.

## What the text says it develops

- Preparation for hibernation, "going into letargic trance".
- The ability "to center the life energies in the head, separating the poles in the body, by closing both currents individually".
- In that state, the text claims, "they may remain for indefinte time, but in reality – 3 to 6 months is used".

## Phenomena and cautions

- The text expects injury: the tongue is to be pulled until the frenulum loosens or is cut on the teeth, and salt is to be applied to the wound afterwards. The author's own instruction on cutting is plain and is reported here as he gives it: by operation, by knife, or with a sharp blade of grass.
- His only limits are "slowly" and "gradually": the practice "should be prepared slowly and practiced gradually".

## Notes for the reader

- The text describes the lengthening of the tongue in detail but does not describe how the tongue is actually turned back and inserted behind the soft palate once it is long enough, nor what is done during the "letargic trance" itself; hibernation is only named as the purpose.
- "Closing both currents individually" refers to the Sun and Moon currents of the body (the right and left, heating and cooling breaths) described with the First Master Arcane; the text does not say here how the closing is done.
- The Eleventh Arcane, Mullah Mudra, is the companion practice "used in the developement for hibernation".
- The manuscript's own spelling is "froenulum lingude" for the frenulum linguae.
