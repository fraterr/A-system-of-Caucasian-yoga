## In brief

Yawning is the body's own way of stirring the spinal fluid "in cases of exhaustion, and low ebb of energies". The author treats it as the model for the whole of XIII L.: the other practices reproduce on purpose what a good yawn does by reflex. A note adds that yawning sends out powerful waves on the ether, which is why it is catching, and that the yawning breath is the best carrier for telepathic messages.

## Technique

1. **Yawn** fully, letting it be "satisfactory".
2. **Attend** to the pressure: the yawn presses on the medulla oblongata, which raises the pressure in the cavities of the head and in the central canal (fourth ventricle).
3. **Listen** for the pressure spreading toward the ears, a rumbling sound and a ringing like delicate silver chains.
4. **Finish** with the deep inhalation that comes with the yawn, felt as pleasantly filling and pervading the solar plexus.
5. **For telepathy**: begin with the yawning exercise, relax completely, then send the message "vibrations on the yawning breath".

## What the text says it develops

- Restores energy by redistributing the spinal fluid to the nervous centres.
- Telepathic sending: yawning influences "the identical organs of people in proximity telepathically", making them yawn in turn.

## Phenomena and cautions

- Refreshing pressure inside the head, rumbling and silver-chain ringing in the ears, satisfaction in the solar plexus. No cautions are given.

## Notes for the reader

- The text gives no count or duration for yawning; the jaw-exercise, which imitates the yawn, is done twelve times or more.
- Two words are unreadable ("pleasantly filling [?]", "in proximity [?]").
- See mental-connection for the author's other methods of contact at a distance.
